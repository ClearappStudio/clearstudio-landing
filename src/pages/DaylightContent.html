A planner. A notebook. Your space.

Structure for your plans.
Freedom for your thoughts.

Some things belong on a calendar. Others start as a scribble, a list or a thought in the margin.

Daylight brings them closer. See what’s ahead, write down what matters, and keep the details nearby — without turning every thought into a form.

01

Your day, in context.

See events from your Apple calendars alongside your tasks and a space for notes in the margin. Your commitments and your thinking, on the same page.

02

Your week. Your month.

Switch between weekly tasks and a seven-day agenda. Step back to a monthly calendar, with all-day events first and more events just a tap away. Choose which calendars to see in every view.

03

Write the way you think.

Write directly in task rows and in the margins with native PencilKit tools. Use Annotate in calendar views to write over the schedule and events, then return to moving and editing them. Switch to the keyboard whenever you prefer.

04

Plans change. Move with them.

Move events to another time or day and adjust their duration with the handles. Open an event to edit its title and details. Changes save back to its original Apple calendar; read-only calendars stay protected.

05

A notebook that stays close.

Give meetings and ideas their own pages, or open a linked note from a calendar event. Add typed notes and drawings, choose blank, lined or dotted paper, and turn selected handwriting into a task. Search your typed notes, or hide the index when you need more space.

06

Small habits. A wider view.

Track routines with a monthly habit tracker, choose a colour for each habit and copy your habits into the next month. Capture unscheduled tasks in your inbox until you’re ready to place them.

07

A little more you.

Choose English or Spanish, start your week on Monday or Sunday, and find a colour palette that feels right. Set your agenda hours, default calendar and notebook paper in Settings.

08

Room to change your mind.

Deleted tasks, notes and habits stay in Trash for 30 days, so you can restore them when needed. Export a backup of your planner and access local recovery copies created during iCloud sync.

A few things to know

Before the
first page.

When will Daylight be available?

Daylight is being tested and refined through everyday use. A release date hasn’t been announced yet. It will be available through the Apple App Store when it’s ready.

Do I need an Apple Pencil?

No. You can use a keyboard for tasks and typed notes. Apple Pencil adds freehand writing and drawing when you want a more personal, open-ended way to work.

Does it work with my existing calendars?

Daylight connects to calendars available through Apple Calendar, with your permission. You can bring your existing events into your day rather than recreate them in a separate planner.

Does it sync with iCloud?

Yes. Enable iCloud in Settings to automatically sync tasks, notes, handwriting, habits and Trash across iPads using the same Apple Account. You can keep working offline. Calendar events sync through their own calendar accounts; appearance preferences stay on each device.

Which devices does it support?

Daylight is made for iPad and requires iPadOS 17 or later. You can use it with a keyboard or Apple Pencil. iPhone and Mac versions haven’t been announced.

Is there a subscription?

The proposed launch model is a one-time purchase of €14.99, without a recurring subscription. Pricing is provisional until the App Store release is confirmed.

Can I personalise my planner?

Yes. Choose English or Spanish, a Monday or Sunday week start and a colour palette. You can also set agenda hours, your default calendar, notebook paper and line spacing. A short welcome flow helps you choose the basics, and you can change them later in Settings.

Where can I get help or read about privacy?

Visit Daylight Support for help with calendars, handwriting, backups and iCloud, or read our Privacy Policy to see how your data is stored and what you control.
