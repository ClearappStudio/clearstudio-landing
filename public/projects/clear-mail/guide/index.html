1. The purpose

Email rarely fails because it is badly organised. It fails because it creates mental accumulation: messages stay visible, decisions remain open and your mind keeps trying to remember what still needs attention.

Clear Mail has one purpose: to stop email from living in your head.

You are not going to organise your inbox. You are going to decide what each message means and bring it to a clear state.

2. What Clear Mail is not

  • It is not Inbox Zero.
  • It is not a system of labels, rules or automation.
  • It does not turn email into a task manager.
  • It does not ask you to optimise everything.

If you are looking for deep classification or sophisticated automation, this method is probably not for you.

3. The central principle

Email is not organised.
Email is finished.

Finished does not mean resolved immediately. It means every message has a clear state outside the inbox.

4. The four folders

Together, these four states cover the messages that should not simply be archived.

Action Items

Messages that require an action from you.

  • Send a concrete reply
  • Make a call or share a document
  • Resolve something that is in your hands

Once the action is complete, archive the message.

Read Later

Messages worth your attention, but not now.

  • Articles and reading
  • Long proposals
  • Messages that deserve unhurried attention

They leave the inbox so you can choose when to read them.

Waiting On

You have done your part and are waiting for something.

  • A reply from someone else
  • A confirmation
  • A future date or event

This removes the thought: “I must not forget that…”

Vault

Important messages you may need months or years from now.

  • Contracts and agreements
  • Key conversations
  • Critical information

There should be few of these, but each one matters.

5. The one rule

Every incoming message must do one of two things:

  • Move to one of the four folders.
  • Be archived immediately.
The inbox is not a place.
It is a temporary state.

6. The daily routine

  1. Open your inbox and process messages one at a time.
  2. Decide: Action, Read Later, Waiting On, Vault or Archive.
  3. Move or archive the message immediately.
  4. Continue to the next message.

Clear Mail works because it reduces decisions. Resist the temptation to make it more complex.

7Use it unchanged for seven days
  • Use the system every day.
  • Do not add more folders.
  • Do not adapt it halfway through the week.
  • Do not create exceptions that bring the noise back.

The aim is not to prove that the system is perfect. It is to notice whether your mental load changes.

7. What to notice

Useful feedback is not “I would build it differently.” It is what changed—or did not change—while you used it.

Useful observations

  • Do you open email with less resistance?
  • Are fewer pending things living in your head?
  • Which folder do you use most, and why?
  • Which messages are still difficult to decide?

Ideas to save for later

  • Adding three more folders
  • Automating the system with rules
  • Comparing it with your existing system

Those ideas may matter later. First, we want to understand the effect of the simple system.

Your next three steps

  • Create the four folders today.
  • Use them for seven days without changing the system.
  • Reply to the email that brought you here with what you observed.

If something does not fit or you get stuck, reply to that same email.
